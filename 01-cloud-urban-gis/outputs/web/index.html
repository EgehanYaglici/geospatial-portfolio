Methodology

From OpenStreetMap to accessibility polygon

Five deterministic steps transform raw graph data into spatially-indexed accessibility areas ready for PostGIS analysis.

01
OSMnx

OSM Download

The complete Amsterdam pedestrian network is downloaded from OpenStreetMap and loaded as a routable directed graph of nodes and edges.

osmnx.graph_from_place('Amsterdam', network_type='walk')
02
NetworkX

Travel time weighting

Each edge is assigned a travel time based on its measured OSM length and a fixed pedestrian speed of 4.8 km/h (1.333 m/s).

G[u][v]['travel_time'] = edge_length_m / 1.333
03
NetworkX · ego_graph

Network search

A subgraph is extracted by finding all nodes reachable within the time threshold from the origin node. Radius is measured in seconds of travel time.

nx.ego_graph(G, origin_node, radius=600, distance='travel_time')
04
GeoPandas · Shapely

Dissolve to polygon

Reachable edges are extracted as a GeoDataFrame, buffered by 15 m to fill gaps between parallel streets, and dissolved into a single accessibility polygon.

gpd.GeoDataFrame(edges).buffer(15).dissolve()
05
PostGIS · EPSG:28992

PostGIS spatial analysis

Isochrones are stored in PostgreSQL/PostGIS. POI counts use indexed ST_Within queries. Areas are computed in RD New (Dutch national grid) for metric accuracy.

SELECT COUNT(*) FROM pois WHERE ST_Within(geom, isochrone), ST_Area(geom::geography)

Spatial inequality

Same city. Completely different reach.

10-minute walking zones from five representative locations. Same travel time, same pedestrian speed assumption, radically different access to daily services.

Sloterdijk has no supermarket within a 10-minute walk. Muiderpoort has ten. Both are served by Amsterdam's public transit system, but the urban fabric around them tells entirely different stories.

Model validation

OSMnx vs Mapbox: two models, different assumptions

The same 5 origins and 3 time thresholds were computed with both OSMnx and Mapbox's Isochrone API. Mapbox isochrones are consistently 44, 168% larger.

OSMnx model
Fixed 4.8 km/h walking speed · No signal penalties · No crossing delays · Uniform speed across all edge types
Mapbox model
Variable pedestrian speeds · Traffic signal waiting time · Pedestrian crossing logic · Road crossing penalties
Origin OSMnx Mapbox IoU Diff
Neither model is "correct." OSMnx answers "how far can someone walk at a steady pace?" Mapbox answers "how far would a pedestrian realistically travel, accounting for signals and crossings?" Both are valid depending on the research question.
Engineering

Technical decisions

Four choices that shaped the architecture, and why they were made.

Network distance over Euclidean buffers

Amsterdam's canal ring makes straight-line radius analysis meaningless. Two points 300 m apart may require a 10-minute walk if no bridge is nearby. Network distance is the only physically meaningful metric here.

NetworkX ego_graph · travel_time weight

PostGIS for all spatial operations

POI counts and area statistics are computed server-side using indexed ST_Within queries. Client-side point-in-polygon loops over 7,583 POIs would be an order of magnitude slower and JavaScript-blocking.

ST_Within · spatial index · ST_Area

EPSG:28992 for area calculation

All area measurements use the Dutch national RD New projection. Computing ST_Area in geographic coordinates (degrees) would produce incorrect results at Amsterdam's latitude without explicit geography casting.

ST_Area(geom::geography) in PostGIS

Analysis separated from visualisation

Routing and spatial statistics are computed offline in Python/PostGIS. The frontend receives only display-ready GeoJSON. This keeps page load fast and makes the analysis pipeline independently reproducible.

Pre-computed GeoJSON · no client routing
Honest assessment

Limitations

  • OSM pedestrian network completeness varies. Some private courtyards, housing estate footpaths and recent infrastructure may be absent from the graph.

  • Walking speed is a fixed constant (4.8 km/h). Actual speed varies with age, gradient, load, weather and terrain, a distribution would be more realistic.

  • Traffic signal waiting times, pedestrian crossing delays and temporary closures are not modelled. This is the primary reason Mapbox isochrones are consistently larger.

  • Accessibility polygons are approximations of reachable network geometry, not exact walkable land area. Buffer-dissolve introduces minor geometric artefacts at junctions.

  • POI data from OpenStreetMap is community-contributed. Coverage and tagging quality differs between categories, transit stops are near-complete; café and restaurant coverage is partial.

  • Analysis reflects a static network snapshot. Planned bridge openings, new pedestrian infrastructure and temporary closures are not incorporated.
