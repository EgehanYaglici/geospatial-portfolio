Amsterdam Walking Accessibility

Amsterdam is full of canals, rail corridors and motorways that you can't walk across. A circle on a map says everything within 800 m is reachable, but walking has to go around. This study measures the actual detour: how much further you really walk vs straight-line distance, using OSMnx pedestrian network routing from 5 origins across the city.

One in four nearby point pairs needs more than twice the straight-line distance on foot. The isochrone shapes reveal which neighbourhoods are well-connected and which are cut off by water and infrastructure.

01Isochrone Explorer

Walking isochrones show how far you can actually reach in 5, 10, and 15 minutes from each origin. The shapes are not circles. They follow the real pedestrian network, stopping at canals, rail lines and motorways. Notice how the shapes get pinched and irregular near water: that's the detour the canal forces. Switch origins to see how connectivity varies. Dam Square has frequent bridges and reaches far; Sloterdijk is boxed in by rail and reaches almost nothing.

05Decisions & Limits
Routing
Network distance, not buffers. A buffer would be one line of code and wrong for one pair in four.
Projection
EPSG:28992 for every measurement. Areas computed in RD New so hectares mean hectares. Only the final export is WGS84.
Compute split
Analysis offline, browser for display only. Amenity counts run as indexed containment queries in PostGIS. The page loads finished GeoJSON.
Buffer width
15 m dissolve buffer. Wide enough to merge street sides, narrow enough not to jump a canal.
Validation
A second, independent model. Running the same origins through Mapbox establishes the size of modelling uncertainty.
Speed model
Single constant 4.8 km/h. Real speed varies with age, gradient, load and crowding.
Crossings
Traffic signal waits and level changes are not modelled. This is why OSMnx areas are systematically smaller than Mapbox.
Ferries
IJ ferries are free and frequent but absent from the walking graph. North-south extreme detours overstate the real barrier.
Data
OSM pedestrian coverage is uneven. Private courtyards and recent infrastructure may be missing.
Amenities
POI counts inherit OSM tagging quality. Transit stops are near-complete; cafe coverage skews toward the centre.
Snapshot
Single point-in-time extract. Bridge closures, new footpaths and construction detours are not represented.
